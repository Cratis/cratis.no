# Cratis site review brief

Review cratis.no as a public company and product site.

## The ten-second test

A new visitor should understand that Cratis builds software that remembers: accountable, long-lived .NET business systems organized around **Design → Build → Operate → Improve**.

## Product clarity

Confirm that the site presents three customer-facing offers:

1. **Cratis Build** — the open runtime and operating foundation.
2. **Cratis Studio** — the paid collaborative design and runtime-understanding product, at explicit **Preview** maturity.
3. **Cratis Assurance** — direct expertise, support, reviews, first slices, production readiness, and workshops.

Technical product names should form one coherent cast inside those offers rather than reading as separate buying decisions.

## Claims to verify

- Chronicle owns the event lifecycle: contracts, append, evolution, derived state, reactions, replay, inspection, and recovery.
- Arc carries C# commands and queries through generated TypeScript to React.
- Workbench, CLI, and Chronicle MCP are open operating surfaces.
- Studio is useful Preview software, not presented as a complete production lifecycle.
- Cratis has more than fifteen years of event-sourcing lineage.
- Assurance is founder-led, with availability and commitments defined by the selected plan or engagement.
- The site says when current-state persistence or a smaller approach is better.

## Public hygiene

Reject any page, comment, or document that:

- names or describes an external product;
- reveals internal evidence sources or drafting provenance;
- invents customers, testimonials, metrics, prices, dates, legal details, or capabilities;
- implies product maturity beyond the current public facts;
- implies more company capacity or coverage than exists;
- makes universal license, telemetry, storage, client-parity, no-migration, or no-drift claims;
- exposes HTML comments or references an internal policy document.

## Experience

Check dark and light themes, mobile and desktop, semantic heading order, keyboard use, visible focus, reduced-motion behavior, readable no-JavaScript content, valid links, and the absence of visible placeholders.
