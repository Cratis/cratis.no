Why now

The way software gets made just changed. Most of it hasn't caught up.

Machines now write a growing share of the software your company runs. They work faster than any review process was designed for — and they only understand what your system has actually written down. We believe that in the age of AI, software has to be built differently — and more and more company leaders are reaching the same conclusion. Cratis is that different way: we build it, we sell it, and we support the companies adopting it.

The risk

Intent leaks out at every handoff

An idea passes through requirements, stories, designs, code and testing before anyone can use it — and every handoff is a translation. What ships is an interpretation of an interpretation, and nobody can say where the meaning went.

The gap

Systems that can't explain themselves

Ask most software why it made a decision and the honest answer is that nobody knows. It stored the outcome and forgot everything that led to it. That's an expensive answer to give a regulator, a customer, or a board.

The cost

Knowledge that walks out the door

The rules that govern your system live in a handful of people's heads. When they leave, the software keeps running and nobody can safely change it.

Where it matters most

When someone asks what happened, the answer should already exist.

Any business where a decision has consequences later — a payment, a claim, a contract, a shipment — is a business that eventually gets asked to account for itself. That's why we believe keeping the full history of what happened is worth it in almost every case: nearly every business runs on information, information flows and business processes, and those are exactly what a system that remembers serves best.

Payments and transfers

Movements of money kept as a durable, ordered history, with retention and compliance handled explicitly.

Claims and cases

Files that carry their own history, from first contact to resolution.

Orders and inventory

What was promised, what changed, and when — without a manual investigation.

Contracts and subscriptions

Every amendment, renewal and cancellation, in the order it occurred.

Identity and access

Who was granted what, by whom, and on what basis.

Supply and logistics

A traceable line from origin to delivery that holds up under scrutiny.

Give it a vague plan and it builds vague software, faster.

The problem Cratis was built to solve.

About Cratis Studio

What changes

What your organization gets out of it.

Most tools produce artifacts — requirements, tickets, code, documentation. Cratis closes a gap: from "we have an idea" to "the software does what we intended".

The whole organization can take part

In most companies, the final say on what the software does is the code — which most of the people who understand the business can't read. Cratis moves that authority to a model everyone can read, question and change. Product people, domain experts and engineers shape the same artifact instead of passing translations down a chain.

Software stops being engineering-only

Design and implementation stay closer

In many organizations the plan and the product separate quietly. Cratis gives teams a shared model, generated contracts, and checks that can expose some mismatches earlier. Engineering review still connects the model to production behavior.

Fewer silent handoff errors

Your system can answer for itself

For behaviors modeled with Chronicle, relevant business facts can be retained in order and queried later, subject to the system's retention and compliance rules. That gives reviews a better starting point than reconstructing everything from logs.

A queryable starting point for audit questions

AI helps instead of accumulating risk

Assistants are only as good as the structure they work inside. We provide AI skills and a system AI can genuinely work with: explicit models, guided ways of working, and checks a machine can run. That guidance is still being validated across AI tools, and it never replaces engineering review.

Knowledge stays with the company

When how the business works is written into the system rather than carried only in senior people's heads, it can shorten onboarding and reduce key-person risk. New people — and new tools — can read the same model.

Less dependent on who's in the room

How we help

Get the decisions right while they are still cheap to change.

Most of what we do is not writing code for you — it is working with your team on choices that are easy to make now and expensive to unmake later. Assurance is founder-led today; scope, availability, and points of contact are agreed for each engagement.

A working session with your people — the ones who know the business and the ones who will build it. Everyone leaves with the same picture, in language everyone in the room understands.

Including when the answer is that you do not need us. A review that only confirms what you already believed is worth nothing, and we would rather be useful than agreeable.
