Cratis Studio

Where your team designs the software together.

Most software gets designed in one room and built in another. Studio puts everyone in the same room — the people who know the business and the people who build it — around one picture of how the business works. Then it runs that picture, so what you agreed on and what your customers use are the same thing.

Why this now

Machines write a growing share of your software. They build what they are told.

An assistant is only as good as the description it works from. Give it a vague plan and it produces vague software, faster than anyone can review it. The bottleneck has moved: it is no longer how quickly you can write software, it is how precisely you can say what you want.

Before

The plan lives in a document

Someone writes it down, someone else interprets it, and the distance between the two only shows up months later in something that shipped wrong.

With Studio

The plan is the thing that runs

Your team designs how the business behaves, and that design is what executes. There is no gap between them to drift, because there is no second copy.

The effect

Speed stops costing you control

People and assistants work from the same explicit description instead of guessing at it — so going faster stops meaning accumulating things nobody can explain later.

How it works

From a conversation to something running.

You do not need to know how any of this is built to take part. That is the point.

01

Get it out of people's heads

Everyone who knows part of how the business works puts it on one canvas — what people do, what happens as a result, what everyone needs to see. In business language, not technical notation.

02

Shape it together, at the same time

Product people, domain experts, designers and engineers work on the same model at once — the way you would expect from any modern collaborative tool. Disagreements surface on the canvas, where they are cheap, instead of in production.

03

Press play

Studio turns the model into something that actually runs, so your team can use it and react to it rather than imagine it. Being wrong on a Tuesday afternoon is much cheaper than being wrong after a quarter of building.

04

Change it and go again

What you learn from using it goes straight back into the model. The loop is the work — not a phase that happens once at the start and never survives contact with reality.

What changes

What your organisation gets out of it.

More of your people can take part

Today the final say on what the software does is the code, which most of the people who understand the business cannot read. Studio moves that to something everyone can read, question and change — so the knowledge that shapes your software is not locked inside one department.

Design stops being engineering-only

Less gets lost on the way

Every handoff between an idea and working software is a translation, and every translation loses something. Designing and running from one model removes most of the handoffs outright.

Fewer surprises at the end

You find out sooner

Because the model runs, the people who will live with a decision can try it before it is built for real — customers included. Feedback arrives while changing your mind is still cheap.

Cheaper mistakes, earlier

Delivery gets more predictable

Studio knows what state each piece of work is in, because that falls out of the model rather than out of someone updating a board. Less ambiguity between what was intended and what arrives.

Status you don't have to maintain

The record keeps itself

Anything built this way records every business decision as it happened, permanently and in order. When someone asks how you got here — an auditor, a customer, a board — answering is a question you ask the system.

Audit becomes a query, not a project

The honest part

Where Studio is today, and where it isn't.

We would rather you knew this before you spent an afternoon on it.

It is in beta, and that means something

Features arrive often and some of them move. Parts are labelled preview. We are still building toward the full loop, and we would rather say that than let you discover it. What is not in beta is how we treat your work: continuous backups, tested restores, and your organisation's content kept separate from everyone else's.

Pricing may still change

It is published rather than quoted, and it is per person. Prices during beta are current but not frozen — we commit to telling administrators at least thirty days before any increase, and we are actively asking early customers whether we have it right.

It will not cover everything

No tool covers a whole business, and the last stretch is usually the part that makes you different from everyone else. Studio is built to let your engineers drop into ordinary code exactly where that is needed, without leaving the model behind.

When it is the wrong fit

If your teams already design together well and nobody is asking questions the system cannot answer, you may not need this. It earns its keep where several people have to agree on how something works before it gets built — and where being wrong about that is expensive.
